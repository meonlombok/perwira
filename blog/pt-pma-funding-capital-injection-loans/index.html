How to bring investment money into Indonesia: capital injection, shareholder loans and company funding.

A practical guide to structuring equity, debt, expense advances and international bank transfers for your PT PMA.

The short answer

Before transferring foreign funds into an Indonesian PT PMA, clearly define whether the money represents paid-up share capital, an additional capital injection, a shareholder loan or an expense reimbursement. That classification controls how the funds appear in accounts, how they are taxed, when they can be returned and what reporting is required.

The main ways to fund a PT PMA

Foreign investors generally fund an Indonesian company through one or a combination of five channels:

  • Paid-up share capital: Money contributed for shares subscribed at incorporation.
  • Additional equity: Further capital through a formal, documented capital increase.
  • Shareholder loans: Debt that the company contracts to repay to a shareholder.
  • Expense advances: Costs paid by a shareholder on the company’s behalf and later reimbursed or formalised.
  • Third-party financing: Bank facilities or loans from an independent lender.

The chosen structure should match the business’s cash flow and repayment capacity. An early-stage villa construction project with no immediate revenue often needs a stronger equity base, whereas an operational business bridging a seasonal gap can often manage debt.

Paid-up capital and additional capital injections

Under Investment/BKPM Regulation 5/2025, the general minimum issued and paid-up capital is Rp2.5 billion per PT PMA unless a specific sector rule requires otherwise. The general investment plan must exceed Rp10 billion, excluding land and buildings, per five-digit KBLI code per project location. Sector rules and location exceptions apply, and accommodation projects use different calculation guidelines.

Paid-up capital is subject to a 12-month restriction on removal from the company account, though exceptions permit legitimate spending on asset purchases, building construction and ongoing operations. It is not a complete freeze on genuine business expenses.

A shareholder loan does not satisfy an unpaid share subscription simply because funds reached the company’s bank account. Equity and debt remain legally distinct.

For an additional capital injection, coordinate the shareholder resolution, subscription terms, notarial deeds, shareholder register and Ministry filings before sending funds. A bank transfer description labelled “capital” cannot substitute for formal corporate actions.

Capital injection versus shareholder loan

Issue Capital injection Shareholder loan
Investor position Equity shareholder Creditor, alongside any shareholding
Accounting Equity and share capital or premium Company liability
Repayment No ordinary maturity; returns require formal corporate capital reduction Principal repaid according to contract
Financial return Dividends, when lawfully distributable Contractual interest, if agreed
Company tax Dividends are not tax-deductible Interest may be deductible subject to thin-capitalisation rules
Core documents Resolutions, deeds, Ministry filings and register updates Loan agreement, corporate approvals and drawdown records

Equity is suitable for money intended to stay invested in the business long term. Loans work better where the company has a realistic repayment plan. Neither structure allows shareholders unrestricted personal access to company cash.

How to document a shareholder loan

Agree on all loan terms in writing before money is transferred. A sound agreement clearly sets out:

  • Lender and borrower identity, tax residence, loan amount and currency.
  • Commercial purpose and drawdown procedure.
  • Interest rate—or express interest-free treatment—and its commercial justification.
  • Maturity date, repayment schedule and early-repayment terms.
  • Security, subordination and default consequences.
  • Withholding tax obligations and whether interest is paid gross or net of tax.
  • Required corporate approvals, bilingual language requirements and dispute clauses.

Keep a separate loan ledger for each lender, tracking drawdowns, principal repayments, accrued interest, withholding tax payments and foreign-exchange differences.

Tax, interest deductions and transfer pricing

A shareholder loan is usually a related-party transaction. Under PMK 172/2023, the Indonesian tax authority applies transfer-pricing principles to examine whether the borrowing was genuinely needed, whether it benefits the business, and whether the interest rate reflects an arm’s-length commercial comparison.

Interest-free loans are not automatically exempt from review; a zero-interest arrangement still requires justification. For interest-bearing loans to nonresident lenders, Indonesia’s statutory Article 26 withholding tax is 20% of gross interest, unless a double-tax treaty provides relief under the procedures of PMK 112/2025 via Coretax and Form DGT.

Tax deductibility of interest is also limited. PMK 169/2015 establishes a general 4:1 debt-to-equity ratio for tax purposes. If company debt exceeds this threshold, interest on the excess is non-deductible. Reporting private foreign debt to the Directorate General of Taxes (DJP) is also mandatory for borrowing costs to remain deductible.

Expense reimbursements and third-party financing

When a foreign shareholder personally pays a company expense—such as architectural drawings or supplier advances—that payment does not automatically become equity or a formal loan.

Retain the invoice, contract, proof of payment and written company confirmation. The accountant must record whether the company recognises an asset or expense and a matching payable to the shareholder. If such advances become substantial or frequent, formalise them as financing rather than leaving an unverified shareholder balance.

How to transfer foreign investment money into Indonesia

  1. Classify the funds first

    Confirm whether the transfer is equity, loan principal or expense reimbursement before initiating payment.

  2. Complete supporting paperwork

    Align resolutions, subscription terms or loan agreements with the transfer amount and currency.

  3. Pre-clear with the receiving bank

    Verify account details, SWIFT code, accepted currency, conversion method and requested documentation.

  4. Transfer directly to the company account

    Send funds from the documented shareholder’s or lender’s account to avoid third-party compliance delays.

  5. Reconcile immediately

    Match the incoming bank credit advice, exchange rate and accounting ledger entries.

Under Bank Indonesia regulations, foreign-loan proceeds must generally be received through a licensed foreign-exchange bank in Indonesia with complete underlying transaction information.

Foreign borrowing brings separate reporting obligations

Borrowing from a nonresident—even a majority shareholder—qualifies as external debt. Bank Indonesia’s LLD reporting framework requires regular reporting of loan contracts, planned drawdowns, actual receipts, repayments and outstanding debt balances.

Foreign-currency external debt may also trigger Bank Indonesia prudential regulations regarding hedging ratios, liquidity ratios and credit ratings. Do not assume that shareholder funding is exempt from these requirements.

Keep corporate records, accounts and LKPM consistent

Ensure that financing records align across four areas: notarial deeds, accounting ledgers, bank statements and quarterly investment activity reports (LKPM). Under Investment/BKPM Regulation 5/2025, LKPM submissions follow strict quarterly deadlines (15 April, 15 July, 15 October and 15 January).

Never double-count financing in LKPM reports—count funds when realised as qualified project expenditure, not once upon receipt and again upon spending.

Two Lombok funding examples

Kuta villa development

After meeting mandatory capital requirements, shareholders provide a Rp3 billion staged construction loan. Drawdowns match milestones, contractor invoices are retained, and repayment schedules align with future operational revenue.

Senggigi business purchase

A buyer pays Rp2 billion to an existing shareholder for their shares. The company receives no new cash. A separate capital injection or loan is required to fund subsequent property renovations.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Calling every incoming transfer “capital” on the bank transfer form.
  • Mixing personal household spending with the company’s bank account.
  • Recording undocumented shareholder loans months or years after the transfer.
  • Promising loan repayment dates that the company’s cash flow cannot support.
  • Treating money paid to a selling shareholder as company funding.
  • Assuming bank approval eliminates tax, transfer-pricing or foreign-debt reporting duties.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use both capital and shareholder loans for a PT PMA?

Yes. Keep the equity contribution and each loan separately documented, approved and accounted for.

Can I withdraw paid-up capital from a PT PMA?

Not as a loan repayment. Capital returns require formal corporate capital-reduction procedures and regulatory approval.

Does a shareholder loan alter company shareholding percentages?

No. A loan is debt; changing ownership percentages requires a subsequent debt-to-equity conversion.

Is interest on a PT PMA shareholder loan tax-deductible?

Only if it complies with transfer-pricing rules, the 4:1 debt-to-equity ratio and foreign-debt reporting requirements.

What is the best way to fund a PT PMA in Indonesia?

Provide sufficient equity to cover operational risk, document loans with clear repayment terms, and agree on tax treatment before transferring funds.
