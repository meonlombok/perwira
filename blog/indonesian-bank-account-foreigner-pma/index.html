Opening an Indonesian bank account as a foreigner or PT PMA.

What to prepare for personal and corporate banking in Indonesia, from KYC documents and tax residence to capital transfers and company controls.

By Perwira Visa · Updated 12 October 2026 · 12 min read

The short answer

Foreigners and PT PMA companies can access Indonesian banking, subject to the bank’s approval and compliance checks. The important first step is separating personal money from company money, then preparing documents that explain who owns the funds, where they come from and how the account will be used.

Personal account or company account?

A personal account holds an individual’s savings, salary, personal transfers and living expenses. A PT PMA corporate account belongs to the Indonesian foreign-investment company, a separate legal person, and should use the company’s legal name.

Company revenue, capital contributions, shareholder loans, payroll, taxes and supplier payments should normally pass through documented company channels. Mixing funds makes bookkeeping, tax reporting and future due diligence more difficult.

Salary and properly distributed dividends may reach a personal account. Rental receipts belong in the account of the lawful income recipient; a villa company’s bookings are company revenue. Account ownership alone does not authorise employment, rental activity or business operations.

Can foreigners open a personal bank account?

Yes, although eligibility and documents vary by bank, product and onboarding channel. Branch staff can request additional evidence. Published BCA requirements distinguish resident foreigners using KITAS or KITAP documents from non-residents needing other compliance documentation. Mandiri’s passport-based Livin’ onboarding similarly expects resident foreigners to hold valid ITAS, ITAP or Golden Visa documentation.

A KITAS does not guarantee approval. A bank still assesses the customer, product, account purpose and source of funds.

Can a tourist open an account?

Some banks offer routes for non-resident foreigners, but this is not a promise that every tourist or visit-visa holder qualifies. Ask about the exact product and visa before going to a branch. A resident-focused app may reject an application that needs branch assessment.

Prepare before you visit

  • Valid passport and current immigration or stay documents.
  • Indonesian address and supporting evidence, such as a lease, if requested.
  • Working telephone number, often Indonesian, and email address.
  • Indonesian NPWP where required, plus foreign tax numbers.
  • Tax-residency information and expected account use.
  • Source-of-funds evidence, such as salary or savings records.
  • Employment, sponsor or marriage documents where relevant and requested.
An Investor ITAS holder can prepare a passport, permit, address, tax details and evidence of overseas savings. The immigration category does not replace the bank’s financial checks.

Why the bank asks about tax residence

KYC, or Know Your Customer, and AML, or anti-money-laundering, checks help banks establish identity, ownership, account purpose and source of funds. Large or unusual activity may lead to further questions.

The Common Reporting Standard (CRS) supports international exchange of financial-account information. Banks may request tax-residency self-certification and relevant tax identification numbers, or TINs. FATCA creates additional questions about US-person status where relevant.

Declare every applicable tax residence accurately. If a TIN does not exist, use the bank’s permitted explanation process; do not invent one. Citizenship, immigration permission and tax residence are different concepts. Opening an account does not establish tax compliance.

Opening a PT PMA corporate account

Choose a corporate product suited to the company’s transactions, often a giro, or current account. A PT PMA is incorporated in Indonesia, so its onboarding should not automatically be treated as that of an overseas-incorporated company.

Corporate account preparation checklist

  • Establishment deed, articles, amendments and Ministry approval or notifications.
  • Company NPWP, NIB and current OSS licensing information.
  • KBLI classifications, relevant licences, registered address and business description.
  • Current directors, commissioners and shareholders with requested identity and tax details.
  • Beneficial owners, authorised signatories, specimen signatures and required corporate resolutions.
  • Expected transaction values, currencies, counterparties and source of capital.
  • Requested foreign-tax declarations and an internal payment-approval policy.

Published BCA and Mandiri checklists identify core corporate documents, while CIMB Niaga corporate forms illustrate beneficial-owner and tax declarations. The exact list remains bank-specific.

Who can sign and approve payments?

Shareholding does not automatically provide bank-signing authority. Align the bank mandate with the articles, corporate decisions and authorised signatories. Decide who initiates payments, who approves them and whether joint approval is required; do not share a director’s login.

Compare mobile and corporate internet banking, multi-user controls, limits, tokens, authentication and access recovery. Corporate platforms can separate payment makers, approvers and releasers.

Capital injection or shareholder loan?

Paid-up capital is equity; a shareholder loan is debt. Neither is ordinary sales revenue, and a reimbursement is different again.

Before a shareholder sends funds, document whether the transfer is a share subscription, loan or another legitimate transaction. A payment description alone does not establish legal capital. Keep transfer confirmations, bank statements and matching corporate and accounting records.

Article 27 of Investment/BKPM Regulation 5/2025 restricts removal of paid-up capital from the company account for at least 12 months, subject to exceptions for asset purchases, construction and company operations. Check the rule’s application before moving funds; it is not a blanket prohibition on legitimate business spending.

Shareholder loans need clear terms and accounting treatment. Related-party tax rules, transfer pricing and applicable foreign-borrowing reporting may also require attention.

International transfers: receiving and sending funds

Receiving money from overseas

Ask the bank for written incoming-transfer instructions, then confirm the exact beneficiary name, account number, SWIFT or BIC, any correspondent-bank instructions, available currencies, conversion method, charges, payment purpose and supporting documents.

Notify the bank before an unusually large investment transfer and ask what evidence it expects. Do not assume that a transfer below a certain amount avoids compliance checks.

Sending money overseas

Match the remittance to its legal basis: supplier invoice, overseas service fee, director remuneration, loan repayment or dividend. Prepare beneficiary details, agreements, approvals and tax documentation where needed. A shareholder request alone is not enough to support an overseas dividend transfer.

Requirements for remittances and for buying foreign currency with rupiah are separate. Bank Indonesia changed foreign-exchange documentation thresholds during 2026, so confirm the requirements for the actual transaction.

Foreign-currency accounts and everyday rupiah needs

Some banks offer corporate accounts in USD, SGD, EUR and other currencies. These can help receive investment, hold eligible foreign-currency revenue or pay overseas suppliers without unnecessary conversions. Availability and features differ by product.

You will still need rupiah for payroll, taxes and many local suppliers, rents and operating costs. Domestic transactions generally must use rupiah unless a recognised exception applies; holding a USD account does not create an exception.

Compare conversion spreads as well as transfer fees. A change in exchange rate can alter the rupiah available from the same foreign-currency balance.

Your bank account is part of your accounting system

Match transactions to invoices, contracts, receipts and approvals. Document inter-account transfers, classify owner withdrawals and support cash spending with records. Bank statements alone do not explain every transaction.

For a Lombok villa company, reconcile gross Booking.com payouts and direct bookings against platform commissions, refunds, taxes and net receipts. Routing this activity through a shareholder’s personal account makes that trail harder to follow.

Use corporate payment channels for payroll, BPJS social-security contributions, expense reimbursement and controlled petty cash. Personally funded expenses need receipts and reimbursement records; repeated personal payroll payments complicate employee and tax records.

Property payments need a clear recipient

For purchases, long leases and deposits, verify the contracting party and beneficiary before paying. Ask the lawyer, notary or PPAT to explain an intermediary arrangement. Keep the agreement, payment conditions and transfer evidence together; do not send a major property deposit to an unexplained third-party account.

Common banking mistakes

  • Receiving company customer payments into a personal account.
  • Paying business expenses personally without supporting records.
  • Using vague descriptions or calling every transfer “capital”.
  • Paying dividends without corporate approval and tax review.
  • Giving one employee all online-banking control.
  • Failing to update directors, shareholders, signatories or contact details.
  • Ignoring tax-residency declarations or treating account approval as tax clearance.

What should you compare between banks?

Compare eligibility, branch access, corporate banking, foreign currencies, international transfers, transaction limits, fees and support. For Lombok, a suitable branch in Mataram or near the business may matter more than an attractive app.

Before visiting

Confirm which branch handles foreigner or corporate onboarding before travelling from South Lombok or the Gilis.

Ask for current terms

Request the document checklist, fee schedule, opening deposit and minimum-balance conditions for the product you will use.

Set controls early

Decide payment authority, multi-user approval and backup access before the account begins receiving company funds.

There is no universally best bank for every foreigner or company. The right product depends on the customer, the transaction pattern and the supporting records available.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner open a bank account in Indonesia?

Yes, subject to the bank’s eligibility, documentation and compliance checks.

Do I need a KITAS, or can a tourist apply?

Many resident products require ITAS or ITAP. Some banks assess non-resident applicants separately, so confirm the exact visa and product.

What documents does a PT PMA need for a bank account?

Usually deeds, Ministry records, NIB, NPWP, ownership and management details, signatory documents and the bank’s further requirements.

Can I use my personal account for PT PMA transactions?

Keep company revenue and routine payments in company channels. Document a genuine personal advance or reimbursement separately.

Can a PT PMA hold foreign currencies?

Yes, where the chosen bank and corporate product offer them. Check currency availability and domestic rupiah requirements.
