Retiring in Lombok: Visa, Property, Tax, Healthcare and Long-Term Planning

Plan retirement in Lombok across Indonesian visas, housing, tax residency, healthcare, insurance, banking and long-term care.

By Perwira Visa · General information; obtain advice for your circumstances.

Lombok offers an appealing retirement: coastal mornings, local markets and room for a slower routine. But a comfortable holiday does not automatically translate into a workable permanent home.

Before you retire in Lombok, consider an ordinary week ten years from now. How will you reach a specialist, renew your passport or manage the house if you cannot drive? A successful move connects immigration, money, housing and healthcare from the beginning.

Choosing a retirement visa in Indonesia

“Retiree visa Lombok” is a search term, not a separate island-specific permit. National Indonesian immigration rules apply.

Retirement and other long-stay routes

The principal retirement categories are:

  • E33F: the one-year sponsored retirement route. Article 61, as amended in 2024, covers applicants aged 55 or older and requires sponsor backing, evidence of living expenses and income or allowances of US$3,000 monthly.[1]
  • E33E: a five-year retirement route without a sponsor. Official immigration guidance specifies age 55 or older, income or allowances of at least US$3,000 monthly, and a commitment to place at least US$50,000 in an account in the applicant’s own name at a state-owned bank. The funding commitment must be fulfilled within the prescribed 90-day period.[2]

These are eligibility requirements, not estimates of what you will spend. Obtain the complete current checklist, including financial evidence, passport validity and renewal conditions, before committing funds. Older online checklists may quote superseded requirements.

Other possibilities include the general second-home category and family-based residence where eligible. A visit visa can support an exploratory stay within its conditions; its validity does not necessarily equal the permitted uninterrupted stay. Do not assume retirement status authorises employment or operating a business.[2][3]

ITAS, ITAP and spouses

An ITAS is a limited stay permit; an ITAP is a permanent stay permit. Qualifying retirement ITAS holders can seek conversion after three consecutive years under the applicable conditions. This requires an application, not simply the passage of time.

For a foreigner married to an Indonesian citizen, family sponsorship may be more suitable. The relevant ITAP pathway can become available once the marriage has lasted at least two years. A foreign spouse accompanying another foreign retiree needs their own appropriate immigration permission.[4]

Where should you live in Lombok?

The following comparison is a practical planning assessment, not a ranking of medical facilities or property prices.

Area Lifestyle and daily services Healthcare and transport considerations Best fit
Senggigi / Batu Layar Coastal neighbourhoods, restaurants and established tourism services Road access to Mataram; steep hillside homes can complicate mobility Retirees wanting beach life with access to city services
Mataram Markets, banks, shopping and everyday administration Convenient access to the provincial referral hospital and specialist clinics People prioritising services and regular medical appointments
Kuta / South Lombok Beach-oriented living, cafés and an international visitor community Kuta has useful airport access; specialist appointments may require travel to Mataram Independent retirees comfortable arranging transport
Gili Air / other northern Gilis Small-island life; atmosphere varies between islands Mainland hospital access involves a boat transfer; assess weather, night transfers and mobility Healthy retirees willing to accept additional logistics

RSUD Provinsi NTB in Mataram publishes specialist clinic schedules and services including cardiology, haemodialysis and cancer treatment. Availability for your particular condition still needs direct confirmation.[5]

Kuta’s airport proximity is useful for family visits, but it does not replace hospital access. Gili Air and Gili Meno offer a quieter atmosphere than busier Gili Trawangan; island living deserves an especially careful emergency plan.[6]

Illustrative scenario: An Australian couple rents in Senggigi before buying. They test the hospital journey, arrange a regular driver and choose a single-level home. Their decision rests on everyday accessibility as much as the sea view.

Buying, leasing or renting a home

Property for retirees in Indonesia involves different legal rights, not simply different payment schedules.

Buying eligible property rights may provide greater control and a transferable asset. Foreign individuals cannot own ordinary Hak Milik land. Eligible residential ownership can involve Hak Pakai and certain apartment rights, subject to immigration documentation and restrictions on price, size, number and use. Have a notary/PPAT verify the current NTB rules for the particular transaction.[7][8]

A long-term lease provides contractual use for an agreed period, rather than land ownership. It can suit someone settled on a location, but substantial prepaid rent ties up capital. Renewal, assignment, succession and compensation for improvements should be expressly addressed.

Renting monthly or annually generally offers more flexibility while you assess neighbourhoods and future care needs. The agreement should identify repairs, deposits, utilities, rent increases and early termination.

For any arrangement, independently check the owner’s authority, land documents, access and building legality. Avoid treating a nominee’s name on a title as equivalent to ownership in your own name.

Illustrative scenario: A single European retiree chooses an annual rental near Kuta’s services. A negotiated early-exit clause matters more to her than a discounted multidecade lease, because she may move closer to healthcare later.

Tax residency and overseas income

A retirement permit does not create a tax exemption

Immigration determines permission to stay. Tax law determines residence and obligations.

Indonesia’s rules include individuals who reside in Indonesia, spend more than 183 days within any 12-month period, or are present during a tax year with an intention to reside. Days need not be consecutive. Long-duration residence documents can evidence that intention, so staying below 184 days is not automatically a safe exemption.[9]

If two countries regard you as resident, the relevant treaty must be examined. Keep travel records and obtain advice before relocating, rather than waiting for the first annual return.

Pensions, investments and reporting

For Indonesian tax residents, the general starting point is worldwide income, subject to applicable exemptions and treaties. Overseas bank accounts do not by themselves shelter pensions, interest, dividends, rental income or investment gains from Indonesian consideration.[10]

Treatment depends on the income category and treaty. For example, the Australia–Indonesia treaty’s pension article allows residence-country taxation and also permits source-country taxation within its stated limit. It does not provide a blanket Indonesian exemption for Australian pensions.[11]

Foreign tax credits may relieve double taxation, subject to limits and evidence. Arrange tax registration where required and clarify annual income and asset reporting.[12]

Illustrative scenario: A retiree receives a pension, share dividends and rent from an overseas apartment. Her adviser reviews each separately, along with treaty residence, foreign withholding and supporting statements. Transferring savings between her own accounts is distinguished from earning new income.

Healthcare and insurance before the move

Start with your existing conditions, not a general question about whether Lombok has “good healthcare.” Ask a prospective provider whether it can deliver your particular tests, specialist follow-up and medication.

Build a practical checklist:

  • Identify a routine doctor, suitable hospital and emergency transport contact.
  • Confirm specialist schedules and arrangements for referrals elsewhere.
  • Check medication by generic name, strength and formulation.
  • Carry medical summaries and prescriptions; verify Indonesian import restrictions before travelling.
  • Keep funds accessible for deposits or treatment while insurance approval is pending.

Foreign prescriptions do not automatically establish that a medicine may legally be imported or readily replaced locally. Government travel advice also warns that serious illness in remote areas may require medical evacuation.[13]

Choose insurance suitable for long-term residence. Obtain written answers about entry and renewal ages, pre-existing conditions, outpatient care, excesses, direct billing, evacuation and repatriation. Check whether evacuation means the nearest suitable hospital or your preferred country.

Do not assume a standard holiday policy covers indefinite residence, or that holding an ITAS automatically provides public health coverage. Confirm eligibility separately. Insurance planning also needs to distinguish medical treatment from long-term personal care.

Banking, transport and a realistic budget

A local bank account can simplify bills and daily payments. Requirements vary by bank and product: BCA’s Tahapan guidance, for example, lists a passport and KITAS/KITAP for foreign residents. Confirm the current account checklist before visiting.[14]

Keep pension statements and transfer records, maintain a backup payment method, and protect access to the phone number used for banking authentication.

For driving, obtain the licence valid for your circumstances and vehicle class. Official Australian guidance specifies an Indonesian licence or an international driving permit accompanying the Australian licence. A car entitlement does not automatically cover a scooter. Long-term residents should confirm local licensing requirements with police and their insurer.[15]

A reliable driver may be a better retirement expense than relying entirely on motorbikes.

Rather than trusting a universal monthly figure, obtain local quotes for:

  • Housing, maintenance, electricity, water and internet.
  • Insurance, prescriptions, appointments and possible care assistance.
  • Transport, flights home and emergency travel.
  • Immigration, professional advice and tax compliance.
  • Inflation, exchange-rate changes and unexpected repairs.

Test internet and mobile reception at the actual house. Ask about water storage, power interruptions, drainage and wet-season access. Include equipment backups where medical or communication needs make them necessary.

Planning for declining health, inheritance and absences

A home that works at 65 may become difficult at 80. Decide who can coordinate care, whether professional help is available, and what would trigger relocation to Mataram or another country.

Illustrative scenario: A retiree married to an Indonesian citizen uses family sponsorship. The couple also reviews marital property, inheritance and what immigration steps would follow the sponsor’s death. Marriage should not be treated as a complete succession plan.

Eligible foreign-owned housing can be inherited, but foreign heirs must satisfy the applicable requirements. A foreign heir’s eligibility to hold land must be checked separately from entitlement to inherit. Hak Milik acquired through intestate inheritance is subject to a statutory disposal requirement.[7][8]

Ask qualified advisers to coordinate wills across countries, review lease succession, and establish legally effective arrangements for incapacity. Keep an accessible inventory of bank accounts, insurance, property documents and professional contacts. A will does not remove land-right restrictions.

For periods abroad, check passports, stay permits and re-entry permission before departure. ITAP rules include termination risks associated with extended absence, including leaving Indonesia for more than one year; confirm how the rules apply to your permit.[16]

Maintain renewal reminders, address records and local registration documents. Appoint someone to inspect an empty home and ensure bills continue to be paid.

Frequently asked questions

Must I buy property to retire in Lombok?

No. Renting can provide a practical base. Housing ownership and immigration eligibility are separate matters.

Is my foreign pension automatically tax-free?

No. Indonesian residence, the type of payment and the relevant treaty determine the analysis.

Which area is best for living in Lombok in retirement?

Mataram suits service-focused living; Senggigi/Batu Layar offers a coastal compromise. Kuta and the Gilis require greater attention to medical travel and transport.

What should I do first?

Arrange a trial stay, check visa eligibility, price suitable insurance and review cross-border tax exposure. Then choose a home that remains workable if your needs change.
